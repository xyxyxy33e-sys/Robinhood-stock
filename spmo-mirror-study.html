What ten years changes

Confirmed

Fifteen is the right size

+567% against +520% at ten names and +549% at twenty, with the best Sharpe of the three. The five-year study picked fifteen on a hair; the decade picks it clearly.

Confirmed

Cap-weighting beats equal

True at every size across both windows — +530% vs +458% at fifteen names. Equal weighting overweights the weakest momentum in the book, and the penalty grows as the book widens.

Revised

The QQQ edge is not structural

Over five years the book beat QQQ by 88 points. Over ten, by 41 — and QQQ actually beat the top-15 buy-and-hold book. The advantage lives almost entirely in 2022–2026.

Confirmed

Weekly rebalancing pays

The most durable effect in the study: +37 points at fifteen names over ten years, and it improved every size and weighting it touched, in both windows.

Against QQQ

QQQ is the honest alternative: same era, similar technology tilt, a rule simple enough to never need a study. Across ten years it returned +526% to the top-15 book's +567% — close enough that the ordering depends on whether you rebalance weekly. Without that, QQQ wins.

Where the strategy is genuinely better is drawdown: −29.4% against −35.6%, and a Sharpe of 1.00 against 0.90. It got there by rotating — but the rotation cuts both ways, and the two crashes in this sample show opposite faces of it.

2022: momentum had rotated into Exxon, UnitedHealth and Chevron before technology repriced. The book lost 6.5% while QQQ lost 16.9%.

2020: momentum was in staples and defensives when COVID hit and technology led the recovery. The book lost 21.0% while QQQ lost only 10.5%.

Rotation is not downside protection. It is a bet that the last six months' leadership persists — which paid in the inflation shock and cost dearly in the pandemic. It beat QQQ in 11 of 20 cohorts, which is close to a coin flip.

What matters today: eleven of the current fifteen holdings are Nasdaq-100 members, carrying 82% of the book. The portfolio is presently close to a concentrated QQQ with a semiconductor overweight — holding the rotation option, but not currently using it.

How the cohorts were rebuilt

Invesco publishes only current holdings, so every historical portfolio was reconstructed from primary filings. Two document families were needed, because N-PORT did not exist before 2019.

  1. N-PORT for 2019 onward

    Fourteen NPORT-P filings for series S000050154, one per cohort — the quarterly report falling inside each window.

  2. Annual reports for 2016–2019

    Six N-CSR and N-CSRS filings, each a 20MB document covering the whole trust. SPMO's schedule of investments was located and parsed out of each — the column order flips between report styles, so both layouts are handled.

  3. Take share counts, not weights

    Both families report actual share balances. Creations and redemptions scale every position proportionally, so relative weights can be rebuilt for any date inside the window.

  4. Reprice at the rebalance date

    Shares × the close on the first session after each reconstitution reproduces the portfolio as it stood when it went live.

  5. Validate the arithmetic

    Reported market value ÷ shares must equal that day's close. Nvidia, 29 May 2026: implied $211.14, actual $211.14. Exxon: seven filing-derived closes matched the price series to the cent.

  6. Rank, combine, measure

    Dual share classes merge into one company — Alphabet's A and C are a single position — then the top 10, 15 and 20 are held to the next reconstitution.

Provenance & limits

A prior caveat, now resolved
The five-year edition of this study reconstructed Exxon's prices, because its 2026 redomiciliation appeared to have erased the earlier series. Querying the full decade returns the continuous adjusted series, and it matches all seven filing-derived closes exactly. Every Exxon figure here is market data, and the affected cohorts have been recomputed — which is why the five-year numbers are slightly higher than previously published.
Three price gaps, each about 1–2% of one cohort
Dow Chemical (1.9%, 2017 — pre-DowDuPont, no continuous series), Pioneer Natural Resources (1.1%, 2022 — delisted into Exxon) and Marsh & McLennan (1.2%, 2023 — absent from the price source). Each would sit near the twentieth slot, so the top-20 column carries marginally more uncertainty than 10 and 15.
One incomplete filing parse
The August 2019 annual report yielded 87 of roughly 100 holdings; the missing names are small and collectively about 12% of the fund. Ranking at the top of the book — which is all that selection depends on — reconciles with the report's own stated figures.
Price-only, cost-free
No dividends, which understates the income-heavier cohorts of 2016–2019 and 2022–2023 by roughly 1.5–2% a year, and understates SPY similarly. No spreads, slippage or taxes — and that matters most for the weekly-rebalance edge, the single largest contributor to the strategy's advantage and the one most exposed to trading costs.
Twenty cohorts is still a small sample
Ten years covering one long bull market, one pandemic crash, one inflation shock and one AI boom. Momentum did well; so did almost everything. The five-versus-ten-year split in this study is the clearest evidence available that these results are regime-dependent.

Decision

The live portfolio mirrors SPMO's top 15 companies at the fund's own weights, rebalanced every Friday. Alphabet's two share classes are held as a single position in Class A.

The decade confirms the size choice and the weighting choice, and strengthens the case for the weekly cadence. It also removes a story the five-year study seemed to tell: this is not a strategy that reliably beats a Nasdaq index fund. Over ten years it beat QQQ by 41 points and lost to it in nine of twenty cohorts, earning its keep through a shallower drawdown rather than a higher return.
