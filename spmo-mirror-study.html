What the ten cohorts show

10 → 15

Nearly free risk reduction

Cumulative return is a dead heat — +171.5% against +171.1% — while drawdown improves about a point and Sharpe rises from 0.97 to 0.99 buy-and-hold, 1.02 to 1.06 with weekly rebalancing.

15 → 20

Diversification you pay for

Nine points of cumulative return disappear (+162.3% vs +171.5%) to buy roughly one more point of drawdown protection. The Sharpe difference, 1.07 against 1.06, sits inside the noise.

Weighting

Equal lost at every size

+146.5% at ten names, +146.1% at fifteen, +128.6% at twenty. Adding names dilutes into weaker momentum, and equal weighting overweights precisely those names.

Cadence

The most consistent edge in the study — it improved every variant it touched, adding six to nine points of cumulative return while trimming volatility.

Why cap-weighting won

Momentum's largest weights are its strongest trends, and across these ten cohorts those trends usually persisted. Meta carried a 25.5% weight into the Sep '23 cohort and returned +64%. Nvidia and Broadcom led the Mar '25 window at the top of the book and kept leading.

Equal weighting only wins when the small weights outrun the large ones. That is exactly what happened in the current cohort, where Micron (+140%) and AMD (+154%) were the two smallest positions in the top ten — and it is the exception, not the rule.

Concentration was close to free. The top-10 portfolio ran a −24.6% maximum drawdown against the full 100-stock fund's −23.7% — under a point worse, for twenty-one points more return.

The trade-off is visible at the tails. In the Sep '25 drawdown the top-10 book lost 12.3% while the top-20 lost 7.9%; in the Sep '23 melt-up the top-10 made 33.7% against the top-20's 29.5%. Wider portfolios clip both ends. Fifteen names sits where the clipping still costs nothing.

How the cohorts were rebuilt

Invesco publishes only current holdings, so the historical portfolios had to be reconstructed from primary sources. The method is exact rather than approximate, and it was validated before use.

  1. Pull the filings

    Ten NPORT-P filings for series S000050154, one covering each rebalance cohort — the fiscal-quarter report that falls inside the window the cohort was live.

  2. Take share counts, not weights

    N-PORT reports the actual share balance of every position. Creations and redemptions scale all holdings proportionally, so relative weights can be rebuilt for any date inside the window.

  3. Reprice at the rebalance date

    Shares × the close on the first session after each reconstitution gives the portfolio exactly as it stood when it went live — the third Friday of March or September, plus one day.

  4. Validate the arithmetic

    N-PORT's reported market value divided by shares must equal that day's close. Nvidia on 29 May 2026: implied $211.14, actual $211.14. Exact to the cent.

  5. Rank, combine, measure

    Dual share classes merge into one company — Alphabet's A and C shares are a single position — then the top 10, 15 and 20 are taken and held to the next reconstitution.

Provenance & limits

One reconstructed price series
Exxon's market history does not exist before mid-2026 — its 2026 redomiciliation into ExxonMobil Holdings Corp reset the listing record, and no data source carries the earlier series. Its prices were recovered from N-PORT's market-value-over-shares instead, using the nearest quarter-end where a rebalance-date figure was unavailable. Excluding Exxon entirely moves the top-10 result from +171.1% to +171.6%, so no conclusion rests on it.
Two names at the top-20 boundary
Pioneer Natural Resources (1.07%, delisted when Exxon acquired it) and Marsh & McLennan (1.23%) have no data in the price source and would each sit around the twentieth slot. The top-20 column therefore carries slightly more uncertainty than the 10 and 15 columns.
Price-only, cost-free
No dividends, which understates the 2022–23 energy and pharma cohorts by roughly 1.5–2% a year. No spreads, slippage or taxes either — and that matters most for the recommendation, since the weekly-rebalance edge is the part most exposed to trading costs, and in a taxable account every rebalance realizes short-term gains.
Ten cohorts, one regime
Five years spanning a post-pandemic recovery and an AI capital-expenditure boom. Momentum did unusually well; SPY's own 12.3% annualized was strong by historical standards. Ten observations cannot separate skill from period.
An earlier read this replaces
Tested over only the two most recent cohorts, equal weighting appeared to beat cap weighting by six points. Extended to ten, it wins five and loses five, and loses the cumulative race by twenty-four points. The two-cohort finding was a sample artifact and should be disregarded.

Decision

The live portfolio now mirrors SPMO's top 15 companies at the fund's own weights, rebalanced every Friday. Alphabet's two share classes are held as a single position in Class A. Fifteen positions on the current account size is roughly $790 each, comfortably tradeable with fractional shares.

The configuration was not chosen because it topped the return table — the top-10 book did, by 1.2 points. It was chosen because it gives that up for a smaller drawdown, lower volatility and the better Sharpe, and because the margin over ten names is well inside what ten cohorts can resolve.
