A tax asset worth knowing about. Those earlier trades left roughly $3,115 of realized capital losses on the books for the tax year. Losses offset realized gains first, so the strategy can realize about that much in gains before any capital-gains tax is owed; beyond that, up to $3,000 of net loss a year can offset ordinary income, with the remainder carrying forward indefinitely. It also softens — temporarily — the argument against harvesting the weekly rebalancing premium, since the first several thousand dollars of short-term gains would be absorbed rather than taxed.

Rebalancing turnover on this strategy is expected to be minimal, so realized P&L should stay small: this week's single trade closed 0.3% of the account. Weeks with no membership change in SPMO's top fifteen may realize nothing at all.

Notes

Why almost nothing traded
The portfolio holds the same companies as the fund, so when prices move, our weights and SPMO's move together. Tracking the fund's live weights therefore asks for a trade that is already satisfied. Over the past decade the top-fifteen membership changed mid-cohort only 23 times — the set is unchanged in roughly 96% of weeks. Expect most Fridays to look like this one.
Worst and best of the week
Intel −5.4% and NVIDIA −2.5% led the decline; Micron held up at +2.5% and is now the largest position at 16.9%. The book remains heavily semiconductor-weighted, which is faithful to the index but concentrates risk in a single theme.
Settlement
First rebalance since the limited-margin upgrade. The LRCX proceeds were available for the NVIDIA purchase immediately rather than the following Monday, so the rebalance completed in a single pass with no deferral.
What this is not
One week is noise. The strategy's case rests on a ten-year, twenty-cohort study, and even that concluded it beats a Nasdaq index fund by a thin margin earned through shallower drawdowns rather than higher returns. A −3.8% opening week is well inside normal variation.
