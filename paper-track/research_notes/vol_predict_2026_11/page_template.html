Can QQQ’s biggest days be seen coming the day before?

The size of tomorrow’s move can be forecast fairly well. Its direction cannot. Every signal that flags a big down day flags a big up day just as strongly.

6,833 QQQ sessions, 16 Sep 1999 to 16 Nov 2026, close to close. Every signal is read at the prior day’s close, so nothing here uses information from the day itself. The top 100 up days are +4.17% or more; the top 100 down days are −4.30% or worse.

What the data says

Predictable

How big tomorrow will be

Ranking days by the prior day’s VIX separates big days (2% or more either way) from quiet ones with an accuracy score of 0.79, where 0.50 is a coin flip and 1.00 is perfect. Tested forward on years the model had not seen (2005–2026) it holds at 0.78. The riskiest tenth of days by forecast contain a third of all big days.

Not predictable

Which way it will go

For up versus down, every signal scores 0.50 to 0.52, and the forward test of the full model scores 0.50. The forecast for a big down day and the forecast for a big up day move together almost perfectly (correlation 0.95). They are the same forecast.

Why VIX looks like it calls dips

It moves with the dip, not before it

On 91 of the top 100 down days VIX rose the same day (median +8%). The day before those drops, VIX had risen only 47% of the time, the same as on any ordinary day (46%). VIX reports the fall as it happens.

One real asymmetry

Calm breaks down, panic bounces up

With VIX under 20 the day before, moves of 3% or more were down 42 times and up 14 times. With VIX at 30 or more they were up 91 times and down 66. The first crack out of a calm market is usually downward and arrives without warning; the largest rallies happen inside panics.

Scores are accuracy of ranking (AUC): 0.50 is a coin flip, 1.00 is perfect. Realized volatility, which the strategy’s vol target already uses, does the job about as well as VIX. Adding VIX to it lifts the forward-tested score from 0.76 to 0.79. The level of volatility carries the information; yesterday’s change in VIX carries none.

What this means for the strategy

Already used. The 20% volatility target cuts the risky legs when realized volatility is high, which is the predictable part. States E and F hold cash through most of the worst days.

Not available. A rule that sells before big down days would also sell before big up days. With VIX at 30 or more the average next day is +0.28%, and 14 of the 30 biggest up days since 2010 came with VIX above 40.

The gap. 42 drops of 3% or more came out of calm (VIX under 20), most of them in state A or D with leverage on. The latest is 5 Nov 2026: −6.5% with VIX at 16.6 the night before. Nothing tested here flags those in advance. This is the case the VIXY sleeve is meant to cushion, since it is bought when VIX is low.

Possible follow-up, untested. Feed VIX or VXN into the vol target alongside realized volatility. The forward-test gain is small (0.76 to 0.79), so it needs its own backtest before any change.

Same

Size predictable, direction not

VIX finds 2%+ days with a score of 0.79, and 0.79 on the forward test (2014–2026). For up versus down every signal still scores 0.49 to 0.52, and the forward test scores 0.47 to 0.50. VIX rose the same day on 98 of the top 100 down days, and the day before on 42 (all days: 45%).

Different

VIX now beats realized volatility

Since 2010 VIX ranks big days better than 10-day realized volatility (0.79 against 0.76), and adding realized volatility to VIX adds nothing. Over the full history the two were level. This is the case for testing VIX in the vol target.

Different

More of the worst days come out of calm

28% of the top-100 down days followed a VIX under 20, against 8% over the full history, and 53% arrived with the strategy in a leveraged state. Drops of 3% or more out of calm: 22, against 7 rallies. The big up days still lean to high VIX: 69% followed VIX of 25 or more.

Weak lean, not a signal

Big days after a fall tend to be up

Among 2%+ days, 58% were down when QQQ was above its 200-day and 44% when below it. After a drop of 2% or more, the next big day was up 62% of the time (47 cases). This only says which way a big day leans once it happens. Across all days it does not predict up or down.
